# Deadlines and Dull Plugins Outrank Viral Video

The best new clone opportunity for a solo US founder is a compliance utility rather than a creator tool. California's Delete Act requires the state's 600-plus registered data brokers to process deletion requests through the state's DROP platform. That duty became mandatory on 1 Aug 2026, each unfulfilled request costs $200 a day, and the processing cycle shrinks from 45 to 30 days on 1 Jan 2027. A self-serve DROP processor for small brokers is open, because the only tools aimed at them are pre-launch or marked "coming soon". Two more ideas earn cheap tests. One is a WordPress broken-link checker that needs no account and produces few false positives, aimed at sites running two incumbents that rate 3.8 stars across a combined 700,000-plus installs. The other is NICET fire-alarm exam prep modelled on Tree Nerd Academy, a one-founder arborist-exam course with Stripe-verified sales of about $35,000 a month. Seven further ideas pass the niche-incumbent check only on conditions. They include a Google Drive folder-copy add-on, a Shopify backup app that can undo AI bulk edits, a Mac version of a founder-claimed $60,000-a-month Intune packaging tool, and design-patent drawings generated from CAD files.

The viral video creator tool, as specified, is not an opening, for four reasons:

- **The product exists.** Revid.ai already sells a 2.77-million-video viral library, one-click remixing and auto-posting from $39 a month.
- **The original has stalled.** StoryShort's Stripe-verified MRR has sat between $21,000 and $24,000 for about 13 months, on 377 paying subscribers.
- **YouTube has ruled the output out.** Its monetisation policy now names templated, mass-produced AI content as ineligible.
- **Customers don't stay.** AI apps keep only about one annual subscriber in five after a year.

The one credible wedge is narrower: live trend intelligence at the level of the format rather than the single video, with a label for each format's lifecycle stage. It should be sold first as a hand-built report to short-form agencies, before any video generation is written. Every discovery track fell short of its target of 6–10 survivors, for two reasons: the session's shared web-search budget ran out, and Reddit and Indie Hackers could not be read. Several leads therefore still need their niche check.

## Fifteen candidates survive the niche check, and two are clean GOs

The research ran four discovery tracks, plus a deep dive on viral video:

- indie products with Stripe-verified revenue;
- users displaced by shutdowns and price rises, and businesses facing new legal obligations;
- app-marketplace categories with weak incumbents;
- fast-growing AI micro-SaaS.

Fifteen ideas came out of them that pass the rule carried over from the previous round. That rule is to run the niche-incumbent search before scoring, and to kill any spin where a free or sub-$20 tool with large installs already serves the exact niche.

Verdicts use three definitions:

- **GO:** the idea passed that check, can be built solo in roughly a month, and can be validated cheaply now.
- **CONDITIONAL:** it passed on the evidence available, but one named condition must clear before building.
- **WATCH:** the check is incomplete, the buyer pool is too small, or the opportunity has an expiry date.

None of the twenty ideas assessed in the earlier "Clone-First Opportunities" round reappears below.

Three patterns explain the ranking.

**The firmest demand is forced, not found.** DROP and EPR exist because a statute sets a penalty and a deadline. Strictly speaking, DROP breaks the clone-first rule: no small-broker product is making money yet that could be copied. Its proof of demand is a law with daily fines, not a competitor's Stripe dashboard.

**The purest clone-first candidate depends on a hire.** NICET prep copies a business model with a verified $35,000-a-month record into a credential nobody serves well. But it needs a certified instructor, which the founder may not be or have.

**The marketplace ideas have the opposite profile.** Ideas 2, 4, 5, 9, 10 and 14 sit on large installed bases of visibly unhappy users. But no verified revenue figure exists for either the incumbent or the category.

Across all four tracks, the spins killed most often were the obvious neighbouring niches. In almost every case, the original product or a cheap tool had already claimed them.

### NICET fire-alarm prep is the one spin that cleared every check

The proposed spin copies Tree Nerd's format for NICET Fire Alarm Systems certification, Levels I–II, with Inspection & Testing as a later extension. The audience is installers and inspectors whose employers often pay for the credential. The Level II exam reportedly has 110 questions in 155 minutes, according to a third-party listing not checked against NICET ([Udemy](https://www.udemy.com/course/mastering-nicet-fire-alarm-level-ii-certification-exam-2024/)). Its fee is $315 or $325, depending on which page of the same prep site is read ([OpenExamPrep](https://open-exam-prep.com/practice/nicet-fire-alarm-ii)).

The niche search found no free or sub-$20 tool with large installs or reviews. It found five incumbents:

- **National Training Center:** $195 for 30 days of Level II Inspection & Testing training, or $300 for 90 days across all levels ([NTC 30-day](https://nationaltrainingcenter.com/product/nicet-fire-alarm-it-online-training-level-ii-30-days); [NTC 90-day](https://nationaltrainingcenter.com/?p=15892)).
- **An "[UNOFFICIAL]" Udemy course:** 4.2 stars from 13 ratings.
- **An iOS app:** $34.99 a year, with no ratings yet ([App listing](https://appshunter.io/ios/app/nicet-fire-alarm-exam-prep/id6779962901)).
- **A study-guide audiobook.**
- **OpenExamPrep:** free question banks.

None offers a course taught by a subject-matter expert with a pass guarantee.

The structural threat is OpenExamPrep. It claims about 23,588 exams, 2,081 of them in skilled trades, "free forever," with 10 free AI interactions a day ([OpenExamPrep](https://open-exam-prep.com)). That pushes the value of any niche certification product away from question volume and toward instruction, the guarantee and community.

The platform takes two to four weeks to build. The content is the bottleneck: a NICET-certified expert has to write or verify every lesson. Questions may reference the code standards but must not reproduce copyrighted code text.

Tree Nerd's channel is undisclosed. Its traffic, high against a domain rating of 7, suggests trade communities, YouTube or associations; a newcomer would reach fire-alarm installers through the same routes and through employer licences. No complaint evidence was captured because Reddit could not be read, and NICET's exam volumes remain unknown.

The verdict is CONDITIONAL. It becomes a GO only if the founder is, or can sign, a credentialed co-author.

## Compliance deadlines create the cleanest gap, while price hikes and shutdowns create none

### DROP gives a solo founder 600 named buyers and a daily fine

California's Delete Act created a state-run deletion platform called DROP. Consumers could file from January 2026. From 1 Aug 2026, registered data brokers must download the deletion lists and process them at least every 45 days. AB 883 shortens that to 30 days from 1 Jan 2027.

Processing has four steps: download the lists, standardise and hash the broker's own records, match them, and report a status for each request. The penalties are **$200 per day for each deletion request not fulfilled**, plus $200 a day for failing to register, plus a $6,000 annual registration fee ([CalPrivacy](https://privacy.ca.gov/data-brokers)). A vendor guide adds the mechanics: there are six standardised lists keyed on identifiers such as email, phone, mobile advertising ID, VIN and connected-TV ID; there is a DROP API; and brokers hash with SHA-256 and Base64 before matching ([UnsubCentral](https://www.unsubcentral.com/california-drop-and-the-delete-act-operational-guide-for-data-brokers/)). The Act gives consumers no private right of action, and no cure period applies ([Clym](https://www.clym.io/blog/california-drop-compliance-data-brokers)).

The obligated group is small, named and lagging. All of these figures come from vendors, and the week-one figure was not checked against the regulator:

| Measure | Figure | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Active registered data brokers, July 2026 | 600+ | [UnsubCentral](https://www.unsubcentral.com/california-drop-and-the-delete-act-operational-guide-for-data-brokers/) |
| Consumers who had filed, July 2026 | 325,000+ | [UnsubCentral](https://www.unsubcentral.com/california-drop-and-the-delete-act-operational-guide-for-data-brokers/) |
| Deletion requests filed, August 2026 | ~450,000 (vendor citing IAPP coverage of a regulator board meeting) | [Clym](https://www.clym.io/blog/california-drop-compliance-data-brokers) |
| Share of brokers processing requests in week one | About 30% | [Clym](https://www.clym.io/blog/california-drop-compliance-data-brokers) |

The tools for small brokers are absent or unfinished:

- **TrustArc** publishes guidance and sells to enterprises ([TrustArc](https://trustarc.com/resource/california-delete-act-drop-platform-data-brokers/)).
- **UnsubCentral** runs suppression lists today, but marks DROP intake, matching, status tracking and auditable reporting as **"coming soon"**. It also concedes that smaller brokers "may start by handling DROP manually."
- **DropAudit** says it is built for small and mid-size brokers. Its live page reads "pre-launch and is not yet accepting customer data or payment," though a search snippet of the same page showed $499 and $1,499 monthly tiers ([DropAudit](https://dropaudit-co.pages.dev/pricing)).
- **BizSuite** advertises $497 setup plus $49 a month, but only in its author's own posts, and it may be a removal service for consumers rather than a processor for brokers ([DEV post](https://dev.to/t49qnsx7qtkpanks/background-alert-got-shut-down-heres-what-every-data-broker-needs-to-do-before-august-1-2g0b)).

The spin is a "DROP autopilot" for brokers with 1–20 staff, priced at $149–299 a month:

1. Pull the lists through the API on a schedule.
2. Hash the broker's identifiers in a local job, so raw personal data never leaves the broker.
3. Match, write status files, keep the suppression list and send cadence alerts.
4. Produce an evidence pack for the independent audits that secondary sources say begin in 2028.

The $6,000 fee shows these buyers already pay to operate, so $1,800–3,600 a year is easy to set against fines of $200 per request per day.

The registry itself is the outbound list. SEO for "DROP API" and "Delete Act compliance software" is contested so far only by law-firm alerts and one indie author's posts ([JD Supra](https://www.jdsupra.com/legalnews/a-compliance-wave-is-coming-data-2897233)). A minimum viable product takes three to five weeks, and the sandbox makes it testable. The hard parts are the security posture and trust, not the code.

The risks are real:

- **A small ceiling.** About 600 buyers cap MRR at roughly $10–40k.
- **Reputation.** Many customers are people-search sites.
- **Security reviews.** Buyers may demand SOC 2.
- **The regulator.** CalPrivacy could improve its own tooling.
- **The clock.** UnsubCentral's "coming soon" is the deadline for entering.

The right first step is emailing 30 small registrants before writing code.

### Packaging EPR and tariff refunds are real obligations with weaker products

**Packaging EPR (extended producer responsibility)** is the second-best obligation. Oregon's reporting portal opened on 31 Mar 2026 for a 31 May deadline ([rePurpose](https://repurpose.global/blog/oregon-epr-2026-reporting-portal-open-may-31-deadline-approaching)). California's SB 54 is the bigger event: first invoices are expected in January 2027, and **only brands with under $1M in California sales are exempt** ([EcoEnclose](https://www.ecoenclose.com/resources/epr-packaging-requirements/reporting-requirements-by-state)). That threshold turns many mid-size Shopify brands into reporting "producers" for the first time. Oregon's exemption is reported two ways, as under $5M revenue *or* under 1 ton, and as under $5M *and* under 5 metric tons ([rePurpose SMB summary](https://repurpose.global/blog/post/an-executive-summary-epr-compliance-in-oregon-for-smbs)).

The incumbents sell by demo. rePurpose Global claims 500+ CPG brands but publishes no prices ([rePurpose EPR](https://repurpose.global/businesses/epr-solutions)). "EPR Manager" is a waitlist only ([waitlist](https://waitlist-eprmanager-com.pages.dev)), and an ideas site already lists packaging-EPR SaaS as a startup idea, a sign that other founders are circling ([liveinthefuture](https://liveinthefuture.org/startups/packaging-epr-compliance-saas.html)). The decisive check failed: a Shopify App Store search for "EPR" returned "We are having trouble loading your results" on both attempts ([Shopify search](https://apps.shopify.com/search?q=EPR)).

The spin maps each SKU's packaging to components and weights, totals what ships into each state, and exports reports in the Circular Action Alliance template, priced at $49–149 a month or $499 a year. It stays on WATCH until that App Store search is rerun by hand and ten $1–20M brands confirm how they filed in May.

**IEEPA tariff refunds** are large but temporary. The Supreme Court ruled 6–3 on 20 Feb 2026 that IEEPA does not authorise tariffs ([Clark Hill](https://www.clarkhill.com/news-events/news/supreme-court-overturns-trumps-ieepa-tariffs)). The Court of International Trade then held on 4 Mar that all importers who paid are "entitled to the benefit" ([AP via News4Jax](https://www.news4jax.com/news/politics/2026/03/04/judge-rules-companies-are-entitled-to-refunds-for-trump-tariffs-overturned-by-the-supreme-court/)). Customs and Border Protection (CBP) opened refunds in two phases:

- **Phase 1 (20 Apr 2026):** covered about 63% of eligible entries ([Endless Commerce](https://endlesscommerce.com/playbook/supreme-court-ieepa-tariff-ruling-refunds)).
- **Phase 2 (29 Jun 2026):** added entries flagged for reconciliation ([GingerControl](https://gingercontrol.com/blog/ieepa-supreme-court-ruling-compliance)).

Refunds are not automatic. Importers need an account on ACE (CBP's online portal), bank details for ACH payment and a CAPE declaration ([Globalfy](https://help.globalfy.com/help-center/ieepa-duty-refunds-via-cape-ace)). About one entry in five has filing errors, and about 1,880 refunds were stuck on 11 May for missing ACH details ([Endless Commerce](https://endlesscommerce.com/playbook/supreme-court-ieepa-tariff-ruling-refunds)).

Brokers such as UPS are already running refund services ([UPS](https://ups.com/us/en/supplychain/logistics-solutions/customs-brokerage/us-customs-tariff-refunds)), and GingerControl gives away a refund estimator. A $149–499 one-off ledger tool is a side project with a 12–24-month life, not durable MRR.

### COPPA, special-district Title II and other state registries remain unchecked

Three obligations stayed on the watchlist because the search budget ran out before their niche checks:

- **COPPA.** The FTC's amended children's privacy rule reached its compliance date on 22 Apr 2026. It adds a written security programme, a time-limited retention policy and biometrics as personal information ([Federal Register](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2025/04/22/2025-05904/childrens-online-privacy-protection-rule); [Finnegan via Mondaq](https://webiis10.mondaq.com/unitedstates/privacy-protection/1790866/coppas-amended-rule-is-now-in-full-effect-what-operators-need-to-know)). The Kids Web Services site returned HTTP 403, and PRIVO, kidSAFE and CARU tooling went unpriced.
- **Title II for special districts.** The DOJ's interim rule moved the ADA Title II web-accessibility deadline for entities under 50,000 people, and for special districts, to 26 Apr 2028, without changing "the scope of the rule and its technical requirements" ([Plante Moran](https://plantemoran.com/explore-our-thinking/insight/2026/04/doj-extends-ada-title-ii-web-accessibility-compliance-deadlines); [Level Access](https://www.levelaccess.com/blog/the-ada-title-ii-extension-what-it-doesnt-change/)). A meeting-packet spin for water, fire and park districts was not checked against agenda-management vendors.
- **Other state registries.** Whether Vermont, Texas or Oregon will copy California's DROP platform was not researched.

### Shutdowns and price hikes were absorbed within weeks

Every software displacement in 2025–2026 that was checked had already been captured:

- **Skype users** moved to Teams Free, with chats and contacts carried over ([Microsoft](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/microsoft-365/blog/2025/02/28/the-next-chapter-moving-from-skype-to-microsoft-teams/)).
- **Gmail's end of Gmailify and POP fetching** sent users to free forwarding, ForwardMyInbox (free) and ChainMail at $1 a month ([TidBITS](https://talk.tidbits.com/t/gmail-to-end-pop-fetching-and-gmailify-features/32657); [ChainMail post](https://dev.to/admin_chainmail_6cfeeb3e6/gmailify-is-shutting-down-in-2026-what-are-your-options-45kd)).
- **Microsoft Publisher's retirement** is answered by Word, which imports .pub files, and by LibreOffice ([Microsoft Q&A](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/answers/questions/5780232/can-i-migrate-my-publisher-files-what-replaces-it)).
- **The end of basic SMTP authentication in Exchange Online** has free first-party fixes, and SMTP2GO is free up to 1,000 emails a month ([CaptainDNS](https://www.captaindns.com/en/blog/microsoft-exchange-basic-auth-smtp-end); [SMTP2GO](https://www.smtp2go.com/pricing/)).
- **HoneyBook's February 2025 rise** (Starter from $19 to $36 monthly) sent users to existing cheaper bundles such as Moxie at $10–12 ([Fstoppers](https://www.fstoppers.com/business/honeybooks-price-hike-coming-heres-what-need-know-and-how-save-690584)).

Several "new obligations" evaporated. FinCEN's residential real-estate rule was vacated nationwide on 19 Mar 2026 ([Orrick](https://infobytes.orrick.com/2026-03-27/district-court-vacates-fincens-residential-real-estate-reporting-rule/)). New York's LLC Transparency Act lost its domestic expansion to a veto ([Simpson Thacher](https://www.stblaw.com/about-us/publications/view/2025/12/23/the-new-york-llc-transparency-act-will-become-effective-on-january-1-2026-with-a-limited-scope)). California's SB 68 allergen law applies only to chains with 20+ locations ([AFS Law](https://www.afslaw.com/perspectives/alerts/california-enacts-first-the-nation-allergen-disclosure-law-restaurant-chains)). Rules aimed at *many tiny* businesses (AB 723 for agents, accessibility for school websites) attract cheap tools within months. Rules aimed at *a few hundred odd-shaped* businesses stay expensive, because enterprise vendors don't price down and few indie founders notice them.

## App marketplaces hide gaps in dull utilities, not on Shopify's front page

### Shopify is saturated except for backup and undo

The research parsed 989 Shopify App Store listings, of which 777 carried ratings. **Only five third-party apps with 120+ reviews rate 4.3 stars or lower** ([Shopify App Store](https://apps.shopify.com/search)).

The worst-rated apps are Shopify's own:

- Search & Discovery: 2.6★ from 514 reviews ([listing](https://apps.shopify.com/search-and-discovery)).
- Bundles: 2.9★ from 571 ([listing](https://apps.shopify.com/shopify-bundles)).
- Subscriptions: 3.8★ from 895 ([listing](https://apps.shopify.com/shopify-subscriptions)).

But each already has a free-plan alternative from another developer with thousands of reviews: Kaching Bundles at 5.0★ from 6,636, and Appstle at 5.0★ from 9,219.

The one third-party category where the leader is clearly degrading is backup. **Rewind rates 4.3★ from 632 reviews at $19–99 a month** ([Rewind](https://apps.shopify.com/backup)). Its newest one- and two-star reviews describe four problems ([Rewind 1★ reviews](https://apps.shopify.com/backup/reviews?ratings%5B%5D=1&sort_by=newest)):

- **Rising prices.** The entry plan went from $9 to $19, and one review complains "they've doubled the cost."
- **Charges after cancelling.** Several reviewers say billing continued after they cancelled.
- **Slow restores.** One restore was "blocked for 4 days" with no weekend help.
- **All-or-nothing restores.** "You either restore ALL blog posts or none."

Rewind has itself conceded that it cannot back up inventory or manual sort orders because of Shopify API limits, and it now pitches itself as "the safety net for AI Toolkit, Sidekick, and Magic," Shopify's AI editing tools.

Rivals exist but none is large:

- BackupMaster: 4.8★ from 124 reviews, free or $19 a month ([BackupMaster](https://apps.shopify.com/backup-master)).
- TinyBackup: 5.0★ from 59, $11–33 ([TinyBackup](https://apps.shopify.com/tinybackup)).
- Four 2026 entrants with almost no reviews: Avada, Savepoint, StoreVault and StoreArk, from $4.50 ([search](https://apps.shopify.com/search?q=backup+restore)).

The idea therefore passes the letter of the check, but the window is closing. Shopify's revenue share helps: 0% on a developer's first $1M of lifetime app revenue counted from 2025, then 15% ([Shopify dev docs](https://shopify.dev/docs/apps/launch/distribution/revenue-share)).

Chrome produced no survivor. GoFullPage rates 3.4★ across 11M users and FireShot 2.1★ across 3M ([GoFullPage](https://chromewebstore.google.com/detail/fdpohaocaechififmbbbbbknoalclacl); [FireShot](https://chromewebstore.google.com/detail/mcbpblocgmgfnpjjppndjkmgjaogfceg)). But Scrnli covers the same capture job at 4.8★ from 11K ratings and 1M users ([Scrnli](https://chromewebstore.google.com/detail/ijejnggjjphlenbhmjhhgcdpehhacaal)). Historical rating trends could not be checked because Chrome-Stats returned 403.

### WordPress's two broken-link checkers share 700,000 installs and a 3.8 rating

The two dominant WordPress broken-link checkers both sit at 3.8 stars:

- **WPMU DEV's checker:** 500K+ active installs and 605 ratings, 134 of them one star ([wp.org](https://wordpress.org/plugins/broken-link-checker/)).
- **AIOSEO's checker:** 200K+ installs and 87 ratings, 22 of them one star ([wp.org](https://wordpress.org/plugins/broken-link-checker-seo/)).

Complaints from 2026 are specific and recent ([WPMU reviews](https://wordpress.org/support/plugin/broken-link-checker/reviews/); [AIOSEO reviews](https://wordpress.org/support/plugin/broken-link-checker-seo/reviews/)):

- **False positives:** "Hundreds of false positives… it unlinked EVERY link (300+)" (29 Oct), and "Every broken link reported is not broken" (AIOSEO, 10 Jun).
- **Corrupted data:** an update corrupted database tables (2 Sep).
- **Forced sign-ups:** the AIOSEO plugin "Does not function without account/credit card" (29 Sep).

A wordpress.org search turns up only tiny dedicated alternatives, with 2,000 and 1,000 installs ([wp.org API](https://api.wordpress.org/plugins/info/1.2/?action=query_plugins&request[search]=broken%20links)). The spin is a self-contained local scanner. It would re-check links automatically (falling back from a HEAD to a GET request, and detecting 403/429 errors and bot walls) before calling them broken, apply bulk actions only to selected rows, and restore access for editors. It would be a free plugin with a paid tier for scheduling and multisite.

It takes two to four weeks to build and distributes free through wordpress.org. Its weakness is that both incumbents earn through upsells whose revenue is unknown, and WordPress buyers are price-sensitive.

### Google Workspace utilities from solo developers are neglected

Google Workspace holds the clearest cluster of neglected paid utilities, several from one solo developer. Amit Agarwal's Digital Inspiration runs add-ons with 400K to 21M installs on "free with paid features" pricing. Some are breaking: Email Studio fell to 2.6★ after Google retired the Rhino runtime in Apps Script, its scripting platform ([Email Studio](https://workspace.google.com/marketplace/app/email_studio_for_gmail/60106804857)).

Three survivors came out of the scan:

- **Drive folder copy.** Lincoln Apps' Copy Folder rates 3.3★ from 131 reviews across 661K+ installs ([listing](https://workspace.google.com/marketplace/app/copy_folder_in_google_drive/891769470715)). Its 2026 reviews call it "a ripoff plugin with ripoff prices" and "very hard… to cancel," and its developer concedes "there are free alternatives, including custom scripts." Searches for copy and duplicate tools surface no well-rated dedicated rival ([search](https://workspace.google.com/marketplace/search/copy%20folder)).
- **Sheets ↔ Calendar sync.** Sheets2GCal rates 3.2★ across 8M+ installs ([listing](https://workspace.google.com/marketplace/app/sheets2gcal/137564231775)). Sheet.Cal is a credible small rival at 4.5★ with 25K+ installs ([search](https://workspace.google.com/marketplace/search/sheets%20to%20calendar)), so a spin must win on timesheets rather than on sync.
- **Gmail address extraction.** Email Address Extractor rates 3.4★ across 404K+ installs, with reviews such as "Doesn't do what it says" ([listing](https://workspace.google.com/marketplace/app/email_address_extractor/1045030766919)).

Google's rules shape the build. Apps that use restricted scopes, such as full Gmail or Drive access, must pass verification. Apps that handle restricted data "from or through a third-party server must undergo an annual security assessment" ([Google](https://developers.google.com/identity/protocols/oauth2/production-readiness/restricted-scope-verification)). Add-ons that run entirely inside Apps Script appear to avoid that assessment, which would be a structural advantage for a solo founder; this needs confirming. Bundling the three utilities under one publisher would share the verification cost, which is how Digital Inspiration operates.

### Atlassian pays per seat, but its open slots show little willingness to pay

Atlassian prices per user, so even simple chat connectors cost a 100-user team $900–3,800 a year, among them WISOFT's Slack connector ([listing](https://marketplace.atlassian.com/apps/1214122/slack-connector-for-jira)). Google's own Google Chat app for Jira rates 2.3★ across 10,994 installs, and its last cloud release came in November 2021 ([listing](https://marketplace.atlassian.com/apps/1218638/google-chat-for-jira-cloud)).

Two paid rivals already serve the gap, though:

- Canary Apps: 121 installs, $1,200 a year at 100 users ([Canary](https://marketplace.atlassian.com/apps/1233261/google-chat-for-jira)).
- Move Work Forward: 407 installs ([MWF](https://marketplace.atlassian.com/apps/1233491/advanced-google-chat-for-jira)).

Their small bases suggest buyers won't pay much here. Demand for Gantt charts is strong, with BigGantt at 3.46★ and $2,090 a year at 100 users, but robust dependency scheduling is not a two-to-six-week solo build ([BigGantt](https://marketplace.atlassian.com/apps/1213016/biggantt-gantt-chart-for-jira)).

The marketplace lesson is consistent. Shopify and Chrome have high visibility and heavily solicited reviews, and both are competed away. Gaps survive in duller utility niches where the incumbent is a single developer, an abandoned vendor integration or a roll-up.

## AI-native micro-SaaS: bundling has absorbed most professional niches

### Verified fast growers sell to a named profession

Fast, durable AI revenue is rare even among verified startups. An aggregator reports a $1,539 average MRR across 1,441 AI startups on TrustMRR, and a $177 median among those under $5k (third-party aggregation; [BigIdeasDB](https://bigideasdb.com/trustmrr)). The 2025–26 products that reached real revenue within 5–14 months share a pattern. Each serves a named professional, runs inside that profession's workflow, and produces output with format rules that general chatbots fail.

### Voice and document extraction have excellent margins and no open niches

**Voice agents.** All-in API cost runs $0.07–0.31 a minute. - **Trades:** bundled by Jobber, Housecall Pro and ServiceTitan.

- Loss runs: LossRunGuru ([about](https://lossrunguru.pages.dev/about)).

- Text-message court exhibits: TextTimeline ([LawNext](https://directory.lawnext.com/products/text-timeline/)).
- Auction cataloguing: AuctionWriter ([site](https://auctionwriter.com/)).
- HOA violation letters: HOALife ([site](https://www.hoalife.com/large-communities)).

### Patent drawings and a better statement converter are the conditional survivors

**PatentFig** prices at $20, $40 and $80 a month for about 50, 150 and 400 figures. It claims USPTO checks are built in, but it does not mention CAD input or design patents ([PatentFig](https://patentfig.ai/pricing)).

The spin turns a STEP, STL or CAD file, or six to eight phone photos, into a full design-patent view set:

- seven views with consistent line weights;
- dashed lines for unclaimed matter;
- sheet formatting and a self-check against 37 CFR 1.84, the USPTO's drawing rules;
- a one-off "fix my drawing objection" product.

It would sell at about $39 a set to Amazon, Etsy and Kickstarter product sellers and to solo patent agents. The incumbents are partial:

- **Solve Intelligence** already generates design-patent views from CAD, but it sells by demo to firm teams ([Solve](https://www.solveintelligence.com/solutions/design-patents)).
- **PatentDrawing.app** accepts CAD screenshots and photos, with no visible scale or pricing ([directory](https://aiagentsdirectory.com/agent/patentdrawing)).

So the wedge is partly occupied. A deterministic pipeline that renders hidden-line orthographic views from CAD is the reliable route, and it is a medium-to-hard build. Complaint evidence is thin. The main evidence that general image models fail on patent figures comes from an author who promotes PatentFig ([dev.to](https://dev.to/local_ai_28441e061d716cb1/i-tried-automating-patent-figure-creation-heres-what-actually-worked-1o0p)).

**A "1% better" Bank Statement Converter** has the best-documented channel and margin, but its slot is crowded. Late clones show it (Stripe-verified):

- BankConv: $600 MRR from 18 subscriptions ([TrustMRR](https://trustmrr.com/startup/bankconv?metric=mrr)).
- A May 2026 entrant: $5.04 MRR on 130 visitors a month ([TrustMRR](https://trustmrr.com/startup/bank-statement-converter.md)).

Three AI-native leads never received their niche check:

- a Hirevire-style screening tool for one hourly-hiring vertical;
- an AI agent inside a niche desktop tool whose vendor has not shipped AI (the 3D-AGENT pattern);
- voicemail triage for a US vertical other than law (the VoxNow pattern).

## Viral video creator tool: Revid has already built the spin

The proposed product would combine three things: StoryShort's AI faceless-video autoposter, a library of proven viral formats from TikTok, Reels and Shorts, and the ability to make one's own version of those formats. That product already exists, it is priced at StoryShort's level, and no one has shown it earns more than StoryShort.

Two things make the full concept structurally risky. The platforms are now actively penalising the templated, high-frequency output an autoposter produces. And AI creator tools churn faster than almost any subscription category.

A narrow opening remains. Nobody sells live intelligence that detects *formats*, as opposed to individual videos, and tracks where each format sits in its life cycle. But no one has yet shown that anyone will pay for it.

### Every layer of "viral format" already has an owner

| Slot | Who occupies it | Price | Status |
|---|---|---|---|
| Faceless autoposter | StoryShort, AutoShorts, Faceless.video, Crayo; micro-clones | $19–199/mo | Crowded; clones earn ~$150–200 MRR |
| Viral library + remix + autopost | Revid.ai | $39–199/mo | Exists at StoryShort's price |
| Paste-a-link "recreate this viral video" | TopView, Rekreate, ViralTwin, ViralClone, Riffkit, CloneViral, Vuela, Zeemo, Media.io, Zawa, ByteDance's Pippit | Free to ~$56/mo | Commodity; named as a category by July 2026 |
| Trend-effect and meme presets | Higgsfield, PixVerse, Pika, Kling, Viggle, Hedra | Venture-scale | Closed to a solo founder |
| Clipping long videos, with virality scores | Opus Clip, Klap, Submagic | $12–29 entry | Crowded |
| AI-avatar UGC ads | Arcads, Creatify, TopView, MakeUGC, HeyGen | — | Well funded |
| Free native templates | CapCut, Instagram Edits, TikTok Symphony, TikTok AI Alive, Veo in YouTube Shorts | $0 | The default for creators who film themselves |
| Outlier detection at the video level | Sandcastles, Virlo, Mysocial, vidIQ, 1of10, OutlierKit, Viewstats | $15–499/mo | Crowded |
| Live format clusters + lifecycle labels | Partial only: Format Finder (static list), Virlo (topics), ClipTrend (effects) | — | Open, unproven |

**Revid.ai is the closest existing product to the proposed tool, feature for feature.** It was founded in Paris in 2023 as Typeframes.

Its library and analysis (all vendor-claimed):

- **Library size:** a 15 Sep 2026 snapshot held about 2.77M viral items, including 2,377,477 TikToks.
- **Collection:** TikToks are gathered "every few minutes from hashtag and keyword searches," and other platforms every 30–60 minutes.
- **Analysis:** a free "deep analysis" of why a video worked, up to 60 a day.
- **Remix styles:** "Same energy, new script," "Make it about your topic," "Post the Part 2" and "React on camera."

Its automation and pricing:

- **Monitoring:** a watcher that flags outlier videos on 5, 20 or 50 channels, depending on plan.
- **Daily output:** "Search & Remix" workers that each produce a fresh video every day.
- **Publishing:** direct to TikTok, Instagram, YouTube and X, with an API and an MCP connector for AI agents ([Revid AI instructions](https://www.revid.ai/ai-instructions.md)).
- **Price:** Growth costs $39 a month (struck through from $99), Elite $89 and Ultra $199 ([Revid pricing](https://www.revid.ai/pricing)).

Revid itself concedes the library "over-represents content that already surfaced through searches." It also says users "are responsible for having the rights to anything they reuse."

Its traction figures point in opposite directions:

- **3,000,000+ sign-ups** between August 2024 and July 2026 (vendor-claimed, [AI instructions page](https://www.revid.ai/ai-instructions.md)).
- **"14,258+ creators"** (vendor-claimed, [pricing page](https://www.revid.ai/pricing)).
- **About $440K of 2025 revenue** with a four-person team (third-party estimate, possibly stale, [Latka](https://getlatka.com/companies/revid.ai)).
- **3.9★ from 306 Trustpilot reviews,** 26% of them one-star, mostly about credits burned on poor output and refunds refused ([Trustpilot](https://www.trustpilot.com/review/revid.ai)).

If the estimate is right, the viral-remix pitch converts free sign-ups poorly.

The rest of the field crowds in from every side:

- **Crayo** packages proven faceless formats ("Reddit video," "Fake texts video") as workflows. It claims $600K a month (founder-claimed, unverified, from promotional coverage) ([Crayo](https://crayo.ai/pricing); [Starter Story](https://www.starterstory.com/crayo-breakdown)).
- **Cloners.** By July 2026 a vendor-written comparison already treated "recreate a viral TikTok" as a category of six tools, including ByteDance's free Pippit, which mimics a reference clip's structure and script ([Riffkit](https://riffkit.ai/blog/recreate-viral-tiktok-tools-compared)).
- **Higgsfield** sells 60+ "viral presets" that it says are refreshed "to match current trends" ([Higgsfield](https://higgsfield.ai/blog/how-to-make-viral-videos-viral-presets)). Sacra estimated its run rate at $230M ARR in January 2026 (third-party estimate; [Sacra](https://sacra.com/research/higgsfield-at-230m-arr/)).

The platforms give the template layer away. Instagram Edits added beat-synced templates that users can publish for others, plus remixing of public Reels, in December 2025 ([FoneArena](https://www.fonearena.com/blog/?p=470794)). TikTok's Symphony Creative Studio is free to every logged-in TikTok for Business user ([TikTok Ads Help](https://ads.tiktok.com/help/article/about-symphony-creative-studio)).

The money in this market splits in two. Model companies sit at the top, with Kling guiding to $500M ARR for 2026 (public-company guidance; [KrASIA](https://kr-asia.com/kuaishous-kling-ai-seeks-fresh-funding-ahead-of-planned-2027-ipo)). Indie autoposters sit at the bottom, where verified micro-clones show what price-cutting buys:

- Faceless Vidz: $203 MRR from 15 subscriptions (Stripe-verified; [TrustMRR](https://trustmrr.com/startup/faceless-vidz)).
- AutoAIShorts: $159 MRR from 6 (verified via Polar; [TrustMRR](https://trustmrr.com/startup/autoaishorts)).

### StoryShort's Stripe data shows a 13-month plateau

StoryShort is the product that Samuel Rondot, the "clone first" advocate, built himself ([Starter Story](https://rosetta.to/u/starterstory/i-cloned-3-apps-and-now-make-35k-month?t=0)). Its verified numbers make it the most instructive data point in this research.

On 6 Nov 2026, TrustMRR showed **$21,161 MRR, $603,950 in all-time revenue and 377 active subscriptions**, "verified with Stripe API key," for a product founded in August 2024 ([TrustMRR](https://trustmrr.com/startup/storyshort)). Earlier snapshots tell the story:

|---|---|---|---|
| Undated, ShipByData | $23,664 | $325,037 | — |

Back-solving from the totals at about $22k a month dates the ShipByData snapshot to around October 2025. That means **MRR has been flat for about 13 months, while subscribers slipped 3.6% between July and November.**

Three more figures fill in the picture:

- **Revenue per subscriber:** about $56 a month.
- **Users:** TrustMRR lists about 27,000. The site claims "90,000+ creators" (vendor-claimed).
- **Conversion:** either way, only about 1.4% or less of users pay.

The plans run on credits:

- **Ladder:** Starter $39, Growth $69, Influencer $129 and Ultra $199 at list. Annual billing brings these down to roughly $23–117 a month.

On Trustpilot it rates 2.1★ from just 12 reviews. Complaints describe vanished credits, charges continuing until a bank intervened, absent support, output that works "roughly 40–50% of the times," and copyright problems on YouTube upload ([Trustpilot](https://www.trustpilot.com/review/storyshort.ai)).

The steelman for the founder is real. StoryShort's problems are mostly operational (support, credits, cancellation) and fixable. A $20k-MRR business of its class is achievable with better execution. But it is a churn-and-replace commodity, not a category gap.

### Trend-intelligence tools stop at the video, not the format

Tools that find outlier short-form videos are crowded, and most of them stop at scripts:

- **Sandcastles** finds "top 1% outliers" from "millions of validated outliers" across Instagram, TikTok and Shorts. It costs $49, $99 or $499 a month ($39–399 billed yearly) and claims 100k+ users (vendor-claimed). It has no video generation ([Sandcastles](https://sandcastles.ai/)).
- **Virlo** charges $49 for Starter and $199 for Pro, with three seats and Slack and webhook alerts ([Virlo pricing](https://virlo.ai/pricing)).
- **Mysocial** scores outliers against a creator's median over recent posts, at $49–349 ([Mysocial](https://mysocial.io/outliers/)).
- **vidIQ** added Instagram on 5 May 2026 and lists an AI video generator and clipping at $16.58–39 a month ([vidIQ updates](https://vidiq.com/blog/category/product-updates/); [vidIQ pricing](https://vidiq.com/pricing/)).
- **1of10, OutlierKit and Viewstats** remain YouTube-only ([1of10](https://1of10.com/pricing); [OutlierKit](https://outlierkit.com/pricing); [Viewstats](https://www.viewstats.com/pricing)).
- **Brand-grade listening** costs more: Exolyt charges $400–950 a month ([Exolyt](https://exolyt.com/pricing)) and Dash Social starts at $999 ([Dash Social](https://www.dashsocial.com/features/tiktok-trending-sounds)).
- **At the cheap end,** Hookest's hook library costs $9 and was rated 6.5/10 as "small and Instagram-heavy" ([Makerstack](https://makerstack.co/reviews/hookest-review/)). Daily trend newsletters sell for about $20 ([Fox Creative](https://thefoxcreative.gumroad.com/l/TiktokTrends)).

Virlo's figures carry the most useful signal. They show that agencies, not solo creators, convert. Virlo launched on 1 Mar 2025 and by 2 Jan 2026 reported 62,115 registered users and 3,261 paid subscribers, a 5.25% conversion rate (vendor-claimed; [Virlo year in review](https://virlo.ai/blog/virlo-year-in-review)). Its founder reports growing from about $1k MRR to $31k after "agencies seemed to like the early product most" and the offer was rebuilt around agency research workflows (founder-claimed; [Indie Hackers newsletter](https://www.sequenzy.com/api/email-examples/ulhbd2932zyfpqf3xtceq8ly/html)). The two numbers don't reconcile: 3,261 subscribers at $49 would imply about $160k MRR, so the $31k figure is probably older or reflects cheaper legacy plans.

No one sells format clusters with lifecycle labels:

- **Format Finder** sells a *static* list of 65+ named formats at $29–97, and attacks Sandcastles for having "No curated format library" ([Format Finder](https://formatfinder.onepeakcreative.com/resources/compare/format-finder-vs-sandcastles-ai)).
- **Virlo** clusters by topic and narrative, not by format.
- **ClipTrend**, which turns trending AI effects into templates, launched on Product Hunt to zero upvotes ([Product Hunt](https://www.producthunt.com/products/cliptrend-ai)).

Tool manifests observed during this research suggest that vidIQ and Higgsfield already wire together outlier search, generation and direct publishing behind their APIs. This was not confirmed on their public pages, but it means the incumbents with the most data are moving toward the full loop.

### Utility and story formats last; half of trends die within five days

Platform evidence ranking named formats against each other does not exist. The durable signals are coarse:

- **Trend lifespan.** A Publicis Groupe white paper co-produced with TikTok tracked six months of TikTok's top 200 trending hashtags across seven markets. **48% of trends faded within five days and only 26.7% survived beyond 14 days.** The "utility hack," practical content people save, was the longest-lived type ([Campaign Asia](https://www.campaignasia.com/article/publicis-and-tiktok-crack-the-code-on-why-only-27-trends-survive-beyond-two-weeks/4bj3k6n8htrywyqvppeyk43j5r); [Pulse.bot](https://www.pulse.bot/media/news/only-a-fraction-of-tiktok-trends-last-beyond-two-weeks-publicis-groupe-finds-7f651586-7dc4-4082-9a25-efea55509b06/)).
- **What lifts watch time.** TikTok-commissioned research on *ad* creative found watch-time lifts of 9% for real people, 14% for remixing a trend and 16% for a creative story ([Social Media Today](https://www.socialmediatoday.com/news/tiktok-shares-new-notes-maximize-ad-effectiveness/694989/)).
- **The cost of having no face.** Showing a person early raised "hooking power" by 50%, which counts against fully faceless formats, at least in ads ([Marketing Brew](https://marketingbrew.com/stories/2023/12/18/pay-attention-to-me-report-shares-brand-tips-for-catching-users-interest-on-tiktok)).
- **AI slop still reaches scale.** Kapwing's AI-slop study found 278 AI-slop channels with 63B+ views and an estimated ~$117M a year in revenue (vendor study using third-party estimates). 21% of the first 500 Shorts served to a new account were AI-generated ([The Decoder](https://the-decoder.com/one-in-five-youtube-shorts-shown-to-new-users-is-ai-generated-slop-study-finds/)).
- **But enforcement is pruning it.** 16 of the top 100 such channels were later found deleted or emptied ([Daily Dot](https://dailydot.com/youtube-ai-slop-study)).

| Format | Faceless? | Evidence it performs | Policy exposure |
|---|---|---|---|
| Utility, how-to, explainer | Yes | Longest-lived trend type; 5-Minute Crafts at ~28.59B views ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/5-Minute_Crafts)) | Low if it carries real information |
| Original story narrative | Yes | Story +16% watch time (ads research) | Low if the story is original |
| Reddit story over gameplay | Yes | Anecdotes only | High: YouTube excludes "readings of other materials"; Reddit's public API closes March 2027 |
| Fake text or chat story | Yes | Vendor pages only | High: YouTube names "scrolling text… templated storylines" |
| AI history or "what-if" POV | Yes | The POV wave peaked within weeks (Feb 2025) ([ComingSoon](https://comingsoon.net/?p=1924475)) | High: labels, TikTok's AI-content slider, channel deletions |
| Slideshow or carousel | Yes | No reliable data | YouTube excludes low-narrative slideshows; Instagram's originality rule now covers carousels |
| Reaction, duet, compilation | Partly | None | High on YouTube and in TikTok's payout programme |
| Trend audio or meme template | Needs a face or avatar | Remixing a trend +14% (ads research) | Lives days to weeks; copyrighted audio |
| Quiz, would-you-rather, tier list | Yes | None found | Medium: the templated look |

Three platform facts matter for any template engine:

- **Shorts views were redefined twice.** From 31 Mar 2025 every start or replay counts, and the 27 Aug 2026 revision aligns all formats on "begins to play" ([YouTube API revision history](https://developers.google.com/youtube/v3/revision_history)). A trend detector should therefore rank on engagement per view or view velocity, not raw views, and must not compare Shorts views across those dates.
- **TikTok pays only for longer videos.** Its Creator Rewards programme pays only for original videos over 60 seconds ([TikTok Newsroom](https://newsroom.tiktok.com/introducing-the-new-creator-rewards-program)), so 15–45-second viral clips earn nothing from TikTok directly.
- **Instagram ranks on three signals.** Adam Mosseri names watch time, likes and sends as the top signals ([Social Media Today](https://www.socialmediatoday.com/news/instagram-shares-algorithm-2025/738034/)).

The product implication is a two-speed design. An evergreen library of utility, story, quiz and listicle templates can refresh monthly. A trend radar must surface signals within one to three days, or most of what it shows will already be dead.

### No commercial TikTok API exists, yet a niche radar costs under $300 a month

There is no official, commercially usable way to discover trends on TikTok:

- **The Research API** is limited to academic and non-profit researchers working "on a not-for-profit or non-commercial basis" ([TikTok Research API](https://developers.tiktok.com/products/research-api/)).
- **The Commercial Content API** covers only ads shown in the EU and EEA ([TikTok Commercial Content API](https://developers.tiktok.com/products/commercial-content-api)).
- **Creative Center** has no documented data API.
- **TikTok One's commercial terms** prohibit "automated scripts to collect information" ([TikTok One terms](https://ads.tiktok.com/help/article/tiktok-one-creative-suite-creator-commercial-terms?lang=en)).

Instagram and YouTube offer some official access:

- **Instagram's Graph API** allows 30 unique hashtags per account in any rolling seven days, after App Review ([Meta Hashtag Search](https://developers.facebook.com/documentation/instagram-platform/instagram-api-with-facebook-login/hashtag-search)). Its Business Discovery endpoint returns media from known business and creator accounts ([Meta Business Discovery](https://developers.facebook.com/docs/instagram-platform/instagram-api-with-facebook-login/business-discovery?locale=zh_CN)).
- **YouTube's Data API** is free. But since 21 Jul 2025 its "mostPopular" chart covers only Music, Movies and Gaming. A new `videos.batchGetStats` method costing 1 unit arrived on 3 Jun 2026 ([YouTube API revision history](https://developers.google.com/youtube/v3/revision_history)).

The realistic pipeline is therefore:

1. **YouTube Shorts:** official API calls on a seed list of channels in the target niches.
2. **Instagram:** Business Discovery on a curated list of a few hundred accounts, plus the weekly hashtag quota.
3. **TikTok:** a third-party vendor of logged-off public metadata.

Vendor prices vary by an order of magnitude:

| Vendor | Pricing | Source |
|---|---|---|
| ScrapeCreators | $47 for 25,000 request credits, about $1.88 per 1,000 | [ScrapeCreators](https://scrapecreators.com/blog/pay-as-you-go-social-media-scraping-api) |
| Apify (Clockworks actors) | $1.70 per 1,000 results on the main scraper; $10 per 1,000 videos on the video scraper | [SocialCrawl](https://www.socialcrawl.dev/blog/best-tiktok-data-apis-2026); [Apify](https://apify.com/clockworks/tiktok-video-scraper/pricing) |
| Bright Data | About $1.50 per 1,000 records | [UsagePricing](https://usagepricing.com/blueprint/bright-data) |

At seed scale, 500 TikTok feed requests a day comes to about $28–47 a month on per-request pricing. Pulling the same ~450,000 records on per-record pricing runs $675–4,500. A minimum viable radar therefore costs roughly **$50–300 a month**, and broad multi-platform coverage $500–2,000+ (estimate based on the listed vendor prices).

US case law favours scraping *logged-off public* data but does not immunise it:

- **hiQ v. LinkedIn:** the scraping was likely not "unauthorized access," yet hiQ ended under a $500,000 consent judgment and an injunction for breach of contract ([Morgan Lewis](https://www.morganlewis.com/blogs/sourcingatmorganlewis/2022/12/linkedin-v-hiq-landmark-data-scraping-suit-provides-guidance-to-data-scrapers-and-web-operators)).
- **Meta v. Bright Data (2024):** logged-off scraping was held not to be "use" of Meta's services ([Quinn Emanuel](https://www.quinnemanuel.com/the-firm/news-events/client-alert-what-does-the-meta-v-bright-data-summary-judgment-ruling-mean-for-web-scraping/)).
- **X v. Bright Data:** X's contract claims were held preempted by copyright law, but its claims that scraping impaired X's servers were later allowed to proceed ([Courthouse News](https://www.courthousenews.com/judge-tosses-xs-contract-claims-against-data-scraping-company/); [MediaPost](https://www.mediapost.com/publications/article/401510/judge-allows-x-corp-to-revive-scraping-battle-wit.html)).

The safe posture has four rules: buy public metadata rather than scrape while logged in, create no platform accounts, respect rate limits, and never rehost the videos themselves. Defensibility then comes from the format-clustering layer, not from exclusive data.

### Stock and slideshow videos clear 80% margins; fully AI-generated clips break flat plans

Each input is cheap:

- **Scripting:** a five-call pipeline (hook, script, scene prompts, title, rewrite) costs $0.013–0.06 at official model rates ([Claude pricing](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/about-claude/pricing); [Gemini pricing](https://aistudio.google.com/docs/pricing)).
- **Voice:** a 45-second voiceover costs $0.01–0.056 ([ElevenLabs API](https://elevenlabs.io/pricing/api)).
- **Images:** a Gemini image costs about $0.02–0.04 at batch or standard rates ([Gemini pricing](https://aistudio.google.com/docs/pricing)).
- **Rendering:** a 45-second render on Remotion Lambda costs one or two cents ([Remotion](https://remotion.dev/docs/lambda/cost-example)).
- **Stock footage:** Pexels footage is free for commercial use without attribution ([Pexels](https://help.pexels.com/hc/en-us/articles/360042295174-What-is-the-license-of-the-photos-and-videos-on-Pexels)).

Fully AI-generated video is the cost that decides the business. It runs from about $0.02 a second (Pruna P-Video at 720p) and ~$0.07 (Gemini Omni 1.2 Flash at 720p) to about $0.11 (Kling 3.0 Turbo) and $0.40 (Veo 3.1 before its shutdown) ([Gemini pricing](https://aistudio.google.com/docs/pricing); [invideo model pricing, Aug 2026](https://invideo.io/blog/ai-video-model-pricing)).

| Video type | Cost per video | $19 plan, 10 videos | $19 plan, 60 videos | $49 plan, 30 videos |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Slideshow + voiceover (~45s) | $0.22–0.60 | 81% / 72% / 61% | 23% / −33% / −97% | 82% / 71% / 59% |
| Stock or gameplay + captions + voice (~45–60s) | $0.04–0.12 | 91% / 89% / 87% | 80% / 71% / 55% | 93% / 91% / 88% |
| Fully AI clips (~30s), $1 / $3.50 / $15 | ~$1 / $3.50 / $15 | 40% / −91% / −697% | −223% or worse | 34% / −119% or worse |

The table shows gross margin at low, mid and high cost, assuming every user exhausts their quota. Its inputs were Stripe's standard fees, $0.50 of overhead per user and a 1.5× regeneration factor for AI clips.

At mid cost, a flat plan of AI-generated clips breaks even at about 5 clips a month on $19, 8 on $29 and 13 on $49. That is why incumbents sell AI video through credits, and why credits are the most common complaint in the category.

Model churn is a second cost. OpenAI shut down the Sora 2 API on 24 Sep 2026 with "no one-to-one replacement" ([OpenAI](https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/models/sora-2)). Google retired the Veo 3.1 previews in the Gemini API on 22 Oct 2026 ([Gemini video docs](https://aistudio.google.com/docs/video)). Google is also doubling Gemini 3.8 Flash text and speech prices on 1 Jan 2027 ([Gemini pricing](https://aistudio.google.com/docs/pricing)). A solo founder should build behind a layer that can switch providers, and re-price credits every quarter.

The honest-pricing wedge comes from this table. "Pay only for videos you keep" answers the one complaint every incumbent leaves unaddressed, and Klap already sells clipping that way ("Only pay for clips you generate"; [Klap](https://klap.app/pricing)). But it is positioning, not a moat.

### YouTube names the product's default output as unmonetisable

No platform bans faceless or AI-made video. But YouTube's policy now describes a format generator's default output almost word for word.

What YouTube rules out:

- **Generic or repetitive content.** On 15 Jul 2025 YouTube renamed "repetitious content" to "inauthentic content," and its live monetisation policy, now headed "Generic or Repetitive Content," excludes "image slideshows, templated storylines, or scrolling text with minimal or no narrative, commentary, or educational value." It also excludes "AI-generated content made with generic or unoriginal templates giving the impression of mass production."
- **Reused content.** A separate rule excludes "readings of other materials you did not originally create," and applies even with the source author's permission.
- **AI personas posing as experts** on health, legal, finance or political topics.

What happens to violators: monetisation can be removed from **an entire channel**, and creators "should not create new channels to get around restrictions."

What remains allowed: AI that visualises "an original character or narrative," and series in which each video has a distinct storyline ([YouTube monetisation policies](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/1311392)).

YouTube called the July change "minor" ([Social Media Today](https://www.socialmediatoday.com/news/youtube-clarifies-monetization-update-inauthentic-repeated-content/752892/)). But Neal Mohan's January 2026 letter made reducing AI "slop" a 2026 priority, even as more than a million channels used YouTube's own AI tools daily ([Variety](https://au.variety.com/2026/digital/global/youtube-channels-using-ai-tools-reduce-slop-neal-mohan-letter-32190/)). Reported channel terminations under the policy remain unverified, but documented collateral damage to faceless creators is mounting ([The Next Web](https://thenextweb.com/news/youtube-ai-slop-crackdown-faceless-creators-collateral-damage)).

The AI-persona rule has a sharp consequence. The highest-paying YouTube niches, finance, health and law, are exactly where AI personas posing as experts are barred.

TikTok and Meta target unoriginality and reposting more than generation:

- **TikTok** makes "unoriginal or reused material without anything new" ineligible for its For You feed. It requires labels on realistic AI content ([TikTok guidelines](https://www.tiktok.com/community-guidelines/en/integrity-authenticity)), and since November 2025 it lets users dial AI content down with a slider ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2025/11/18/tiktok-now-lets-you-choose-how-much-ai-generated-content-you-want-to-see/)).
- **Meta** has penalised Facebook accounts that repeatedly reuse others' content since July 2025 ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2025/07/14/following-youtube-meta-announces-crackdown-on-unoriginal-facebook-content)). Since 30 Apr 2026, Instagram no longer recommends to non-followers any account whose posts are mostly unoriginal, judged across a month of posts ([PetaPixel](https://petapixel.com/2026/04/30/new-instagram-policies-target-reposted-content/)).

The "automate a monetised faceless channel" use case is shrinking. Uses that don't depend on ad revenue are less exposed: brand marketing, product marketing, affiliate, lead generation, and creators who add their own voice or footage.

### Auto-posting needs three audits and breaks "set and forget"

| Platform | Gate before posts can be public | Volume cap | Rules that conflict with an autopilot |
|---|---|---|---|
| TikTok | Terms-of-service audit; unaudited apps post privately for at most 5 users in 24 hours | ~15 direct posts per creator per day, shared across all apps | Privacy dropdown "with no default," consent per upload, preview, music-use confirmation, no watermarks, no apps that "copy arbitrary content from other platforms" |
| Instagram | Professional account; Meta app review for publishing permission | 100 API posts per account per 24 hours (the same page says 50 for carousels) | Media must sit on a public URL; library music can't be attached through the API |
| YouTube | Audit; uploads from unverified projects locked private | 100 `videos.insert` calls per project per day, about 3,000 a month across all users | Enforcement against inauthentic content at channel level |

Sources: [TikTok content sharing guidelines](https://developers.tiktok.com/doc/content-sharing-guidelines); [TikTok Content Posting API](https://developers.tiktok.com/doc/content-posting-api-get-started); [Meta content publishing](https://developers.facebook.com/docs/instagram-platform/content-publishing); [YouTube quota costs](https://developers.google.com/youtube/v3/determine_quota_cost); [YouTube videos](https://developers.google.com/youtube/v3/docs/videos).

**YouTube's quota alone binds early.** About 377 subscribers posting once a day, StoryShort's scale, would already exceed 100 uploads a day, so the quota-extension audit must be filed before launch. This also corrects the earlier round: YouTube's documentation still restricts uploads from unverified projects to private until audit.

**TikTok's rules complicate the idea itself.** The ban on watermarks removes the usual free-tier growth loop. The ban on copying content from other platforms sits awkwardly with cross-platform "remix this TikTok" features. Aggregators such as Blotato already resell posting through their own audited apps ([Blotato](https://www.blotato.com/api/tiktok)), which is the practical workaround while an audit is pending.

### Legal exposure sits in music, voices and copied scripts, not formats

Copying a *format* is low-risk. In *Manno v. Campbell*, a court dismissed a TikTok creator's claim because a video premise was an unprotectable idea and its sequence a stock element (*scène à faire*) ([SDNY Blog](https://www.sdnyblog.com/judge-cote-concludes-that-the-concept-of-hiring-a-homegirl-for-help-in-social-situations-is-not-protectable-under-copyright-laws/)). The exception is distinctive choreography: *Hanagami v. Epic* held that steps "as minimal as two seconds" may be protectable ([Nixon Peabody](https://www.nixonpeabody.com/-/media/files/alerts/2023/12/protect-your-dance-moves-the-ninth-circuit-approves.pdf)).

The real risks, from highest to lowest:

1. **Music.** TikTok business accounts are limited to the Commercial Music Library, which lacks most trending songs. Videos posted through the API must carry their audio baked in ([Soundstripe](https://www.soundstripe.com/blogs/tiktok-music-licensing-rules)). Ship only licensed or AI-generated music.
2. **Voice and likeness cloning.** Tennessee's ELVIS Act reaches makers of tools whose "primary purpose" is unauthorised replicas ([Munck Wilson](https://www.munckwilson.com/news-insights/ai-likeness-protections-legislative-update/)). The federal NO FAKES Act passed the Senate Judiciary Committee on 18 Jun 2026 but is not law ([Sen. Blackburn](https://www.blackburn.senate.gov/2026/6/blackburn-coons-bipartisan-bill-to-protect-individuals-and-creators-from-deepfakes-passes-senate-judiciary-committee)).
3. **Copied scripts and Reddit posts.** These are protected expression, and YouTube refuses to monetise them regardless.
4. **Gameplay libraries.** Minecraft lets creators monetise their own videos that add original content ([Mojang guidelines](https://help-redesign.zendesk.com/hc/en-us/articles/34348618194189-Content-Creation-and-Broadcasting-Terms-of-Use-and-Guidelines)). A SaaS that packages a publisher's footage as a feature falls outside that permission (inference).
5. **Fake-testimonial "UGC review" formats.** The FTC's 2024 rule bans fake reviews "including those created by generative AI," with penalties of up to $51,744 per violation ([WilmerHale](https://www.wilmerhale.com/en/insights/client-alerts/20241009-ftc-finalizes-rule-banning-fake-reviews-and-testimonials-and-touts-new-rule-at-open-commission-meeting)).
6. **Public galleries.** A tool that hosts users' videos publicly may need the 48-hour takedown process for non-consensual intimate images, including deepfakes, that the FTC has enforced under the Take It Down Act since 19 May 2026 ([Orrick](https://www.orrick.com/fr-FR/Insights/2026/06/Nonconsensual-Intimate-Images-Online-Take-It-Down-Act-Enforcement-In-Full-Swing)).

Two background facts affect the claims a product can make:

- **Users may not own what they make.** The Copyright Office found that prompts alone don't make a user the author of AI output, so "you own your videos" is a contractual promise, not copyright ([Copyright Alliance](https://copyrightalliance.org/ai-report-part-2-copyrightability/)).
- **Section 230 offers little cover.** It probably does not protect a vendor for scripts its own model writes ([Lawfare](https://www.lawfaremedia.org/article/section-230-wont-protect-chatgpt)).

StoryShort's terms show the standard mitigations. Users bear responsibility for rights. Platform AI labels are on by default. Likeness and voice cloning without consent is banned, and only the user's own voice may be cloned. Repeat infringers lose their accounts ([StoryShort Terms](https://storyshort.ai/terms)).

### AI creator tools keep about one annual subscriber in five

RevenueCat's 2026 report, covering 115,000+ apps, found AI apps convert trials 52% better and earn 41% more per payer. But they retain far worse:

- **Annual plans:** 21.1% of AI-app subscribers remain after a year, against 30.7% for other apps.
- **Monthly plans:** 6.1% remain after 12 months, against 9.5%.
- **Churn speed:** AI apps churn about 30% faster.

Sources: [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/03/10/ai-powered-apps-struggle-with-long-term-retention-new-report-shows); [ppc.land](https://ppc.land/ai-apps-earn-41-more-per-user-but-churn-30-faster-revenuecat-finds/).

A secondary summary of ChartMogul data puts median gross revenue retention for AI-native SaaS at about 40% ([Artisan Growth Strategies](https://www.artisangrowthstrategies.com/md/blog/ai-saas-churn-retention-benchmarks-2026)).

Applied to StoryShort's flat base of about 380 subscribers, illustrative monthly churn of 10–20% would require 38–76 new paying customers every month just to stand still. That is consistent with the founder's remark that growth "costs money for ads" ([YouMind](https://youmind.com/es-ES/landing/x-viral-articles/claude-code-saas-success-guide)). Demonetisation pushes the same way: a user whose channel loses monetisation or reach cancels. For this category, acquisition cost and churn, not generation cost, are the economic risk.

### The narrowest credible wedge is a format radar sold to agencies

Three wedges survive scrutiny, ranked by credibility.

**1. A format radar for agencies in one vertical, intelligence only, at $199 or more a month.** This is the most credible, for four reasons:

- **It names the open layer.** It sells the one thing no incumbent does: grouping outlier videos into reproducible formats and labelling each as emerging, peaking or saturated, by number of adopters and speed of spread.
- **It sells to the proven buyer.** Agencies are the only segment with documented conversion (Virlo's pivot), and they pay four to ten times what creators pay.
- **It carries no generation costs.**
- **The policy risk sits elsewhere.** It stays on the customer's channels, not inside the product.

It still competes with Virlo Pro at $199 and Sandcastles Titan at $499, so the format and lifecycle layer has to be visibly better than a list of videos.

**2. The same radar, plus one-click templates, for faceless operators in two to four evergreen niches, at $29–79 a month.** This is the closest to the StoryShort spin. Its formats would be mechanically reproducible ones: stock footage, voiceover and captions. But it sells into Revid's $39 bundle and into the highest-churn buyer group. Its niches should be utility and explainer topics outside finance, health and law, where YouTube's AI-persona rule bites.

**3. A generator with honest pricing**: pay only for kept videos, refund failed generations, roll credits over. This is a marketing position any incumbent could copy, not a company.

Three variants are excluded. Realtor listing video was killed in the earlier round. Organic-format intelligence for TikTok Shop sellers overlaps Zawa, Pippit and TopView, and the earlier round killed TikTok Shop research tools. Reddit-story generators stack copyright problems, YouTube's exclusion and Reddit's March 2027 API closure.

The earlier round's one surviving sliver, estimated per-video sale attribution for faceless digital-product sellers, serves the same operators as wedge 2 and could be bundled with it later.

### A two-week concierge test decides it for under $300

The test should start without code, following the lesson of the previous round.

1. **Seed lists (days 1–2).** Pick one agency-served vertical; beauty and consumer packaged goods are the examples the evidence suggests. Build seed lists of 300–500 creator and brand accounts on TikTok and YouTube Shorts. Pull YouTube through the official API at no cost, and TikTok through a per-request vendor. ScrapeCreators' $47 pack of 25,000 credits comfortably covers 500 requests a day for two weeks (estimate).
2. **Hand-built radar (days 3–14).** Every other day, compute outliers against each account's median performance. Group them by hand into named formats, by hook type, structure, text overlay and audio. Label each format's lifecycle stage. Send a one-page digest by email or Slack.
3. **Pricing.** Offer the digest to 20–30 short-form agencies and brand social teams at $199 for the month. In parallel, offer a $49 version to faceless operators.
4. **Back-test.** Check whether formats labelled "emerging" in week one gained more adopters, or more views per post, in week two than formats labelled "peaking."

Proposed thresholds: continue if at least five agencies pay, or at least three renew for a second month, *and* the back-test shows the lifecycle labels predict. Stop if fewer than three pay or the labels predict nothing. Only after a pass is it worth building automated multimodal clustering. Generation templates come last, if at all.

## Conclusion

This round confirms the earlier lesson and adds two refinements to the method.

The first concerns verified revenue. Stripe-verified histories now let a cloner tell a plateau from a growth curve, and the plateau is often the real signal. StoryShort was built by the clone-first method's own author. Its flat $21,000 MRR on 377 subscribers is the clearest sign in this research that a crowded consumer creator category has a ceiling. Revid has the most data and the most sign-ups in that category, yet appears to earn no more. A cloner should read a verified flat line as evidence of a churn-and-replace market, and treat it as a reason to clone less, not more.

The second concerns where gaps survive. They no longer need only a platform or regulator that makes the job hard. They need a buyer pool too small, too obscure or too dull for venture-backed and enterprise vendors to price down to:

- 600 data brokers;
- fire-alarm technicians;
- Mac admins at small firms;
- site owners fed up with false broken-link alerts.

The viral-video case adds a third filter the method lacked: a **platform-policy check**. That check would have killed the idea even if no niche incumbent had existed, because YouTube's own policy text now excludes the product's default output from monetisation. Future idea sweeps should therefore run three gates in order before any scoring: niche incumbents, platform policy, and verified revenue shape.
