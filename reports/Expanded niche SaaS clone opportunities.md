# Roll-Up Neglect Opens Eighty-Nine Niche Clones

The strongest "copy and improve" targets for a US founder are dull, compliance-heavy back offices in field trades and small organisations, where a roll-up bought the incumbent and let it decay. **Lawn and tree-shrub chemical-program software scores highest (23/25).** WorkWave's RealGreen mobile app rates **1.48★**, and Service Autopilot users say its new owner forced them onto its own card processor ([iTunes](https://itunes.apple.com/search?term=RealGreen&entity=software&country=us); [Capterra](https://www.capterra.com/p/122075/Service-Autopilot/reviews/)). **Commercial janitorial operations follow at 22**, and six ideas tie at 21: an agency-grade form backend (contested by another track), kennel and daycare software (Togetherwork's PetExec has stopped taking new customers), grappling academies (one founder bootstrapped Gymdesk to about $3M ARR), turnover cleaning for short-term rentals, vendor applications for craft fairs and markets, and tattoo studios. **The most robust signal is convergence**: three of the ten research tracks independently arrived at inspection software for small fire-extinguisher and life-safety shops, which carries the only Stripe-verified niche ramp in the set (UK-based FireCert went from **$234 to about $4,700 a month in seven months** ([TrustMRR](https://trustmrr.com/startup/firecert.md))) and faces a best-known US incumbent that excludes shops with fewer than three technicians. Septic and grease-trap pumping, storage lots and market vendor applications also surfaced in more than one track, and in total **89 candidates were shortlisted, 20 of them scoring 20 or more**. The evidence is thinner than the scores imply: most revenue figures are Latka estimates (several visibly wrong), only a handful are Stripe-verified, Capterra stars cluster at 4.5–5.0, and the shared search budget ran out partway through several tracks. The practical next step is to test eight candidates with cheap outreach before writing code, using public licensee lists, association directories and migration offers to users of acquired products.

## Twenty of eighty-nine candidates clear 20 points

Ten parallel research tracks looked at roughly 270 niches and products and scored each candidate 1–5 on five criteria: **revenue proof** (whether something similar demonstrably makes money), **beatability** (how weak the incumbents are), **copyability** (whether a solo MVP is feasible in one to three months), **expansion room** (how many adjacent niches it can grow into) and **channel replicability** (whether a newcomer can reach buyers the way the incumbents do). A **kill rule** removed any niche already dominated by a modern product rated about 4.6★ or higher on many reviews, or any feature that core software now bundles free. Beatability was judged on incumbent weakness rather than on the absence of competition, using dated UX, falling ratings, price hikes, neglect after a roll-up or private-equity purchase, demo-only pricing, missing or poor mobile apps, and horizontal tools that ignore the niche's workflow. Different researchers scored different tracks, so totals compare only roughly across verticals.

## Fire inspection surfaced in three separate tracks

The clearest signal in this round is the same idea turning up in tracks that never compared notes. The home and field services track scored small-shop fire and life-safety inspection 20, citing ServiceTrade at about **$30.4M** in estimated revenue alongside offline-sync complaints ([Latka](https://getlatka.com/companies/servicetrade)). The industrial and civic track independently scored it 20, citing Inspect Point's 3.8★ rating on five reviews and its value-for-money score of 3.2 ([Capterra](https://www.capterra.com/p/148287/Inspect-Point/reviews/)). The verified-revenue sweep reached it a third way, scoring it 18 after finding **FireCert's Stripe-verified climb from $234 to $4,696 a month** between March and October 2026 at £79–129 a month, together with Inspect Point's own statement that its plans "start at three technicians" ([TrustMRR](https://trustmrr.com/startup/firecert.md); [Inspect Point](https://www.inspectpoint.com/pricing)). Adjacent findings reinforce the cluster. Many jurisdictions make contractors upload inspection reports through a third-party portal for a fee (**$15 per report in Charleston**, $25 per system in Tacoma, $35 in Worcester County), which hands a newcomer a ready-made integration hook ([Charleston](https://charleston-sc.gov/DocumentCenter/View/29992)). The small-business acquirer SureSwift says it is "seeking to acquire profitable property service businesses, including HVAC, fire safety", so buyers are already watching the space ([SureSwift](https://www.sureswiftcapital.com/portfolio)). An offline backflow-tester app also scored 19, and the two products would share a forms engine.

Roll-up ownership appears behind the strongest candidates in nearly every track, and the owners behave alike after a deal. **They take over payments**: Service Autopilot users say they lost their choice of processor, Storable moved SiteLink customers onto its own merchant services, Ricochet told consignment shops to move to Fortis or face a **"$100 a day"** fine, and Molo holds marina payouts ([Capterra](https://www.capterra.com/p/136226/Ricochet-Consignment-Software/reviews/)). **They force migrations or sunset products**: PetExec stopped taking new clients, ESO is phasing out Emergency Reporting, ChamberMaster users are being moved to GrowthZone, Houzz stopped selling Ivy, and WineDirect clients are being moved to Commerce7 ([Houzz](https://www.houzz.com/for-pros/difference-between-ivy-and-houzz-pro)). And **they let support decay**. Each pattern hands a clone a simple wedge: an importer for the incumbent's data export, flat pricing, and payouts to the customer's own Stripe account.

## The evidence rests on estimates, proxies and a spent search budget

Seven limits apply to everything above.

**The shared search budget ran out.** The session's 200-call WebSearch limit was exhausted in the education, professional-services, roll-up and verified-revenue tracks. The tool's own instructions forbid working around the limit through Bing or other engines. Researchers complied and switched to direct fetches of known pages: Capterra category and review pages, vendor pricing pages, Latka, the iTunes Search API and TrustMRR profiles. That was enough for most beatability verdicts. It was not enough for some TAM counts, or for incumbents not already known by URL. Hence the hold list.

**Several standard sources were unavailable.** Reddit and Indie Hackers could not be read (one r/CraftFairs thread was read through a mirror), G2 and Trustpilot returned 403 errors, the iTunes API rate-limited after about 30 queries, and the GitHub API was blocked, so open-source adoption is unquantified. The Census API now requires a key, so three tracks fell back to the bulk County Business Patterns files, which count only employer establishments and miss solo operators ([Census CBP 2023](https://www2.census.gov/programs-surveys/cbp/datasets/2023/cbp23us.zip)).

**Most revenue figures are Latka estimates, and several are visibly wrong.** GorillaDesk and BookingKoala show an identical "$880K, 8 employees" ([Latka](https://getlatka.com/companies/gorilladesk)). ServiceCore shows "$548M raised" against a documented $54M recapitalisation ([Latka](https://getlatka.com/companies/servicecore)), and Uptick's page says it was bought by "BK Poker" ([Latka](https://getlatka.com/companies/uptick)). Caterease pairs $969.5K of revenue with "30K customers", about $32 per customer per year ([Latka](https://getlatka.com/companies/caterease)); Bookeo claims 40K customers on $246.6K ([Latka](https://getlatka.com/companies/bookeo)); and EZLynx claims 55.7K customers on $5M ([Latka](https://getlatka.com/companies/ezlynx)). Spectora's $5.9M looks low for 12,000 inspectors ([Latka](https://getlatka.com/companies/spectora)), iClassPro's estimate jumped from $2M to $5.7M within five months ([Latka](https://getlatka.com/companies/iclasspro)), and Latka's "Jumbula" page describes a different product entirely ([Latka](https://getlatka.com/companies/jumbula)). Treat every Latka figure as an order of magnitude at best.

**Stripe-verified figures are rare, and even they need care.** TrustMRR asks AI agents not to scrape, so only its public endpoints and the top 60 startups per category were used, and smaller verified startups are missing ([TrustMRR llms.txt](https://trustmrr.com/llms.txt)). Snapshots also drift slightly, and Web3Forms' verified history appears to start only when its Paddle account was connected.

**Capterra stars cluster at 4.5–5.0 almost everywhere**, so headline ratings say little. Beatability judgments leaned instead on review text and recency, value-for-money sub-scores, the absence of any new reviews in twelve months for some incumbents, and iOS ratings, where the real gaps show (1.48★ for RealGreen, 1.31★ for MyOmegaFi).

**Several key facts come from search summaries rather than fetched pages**, including PetExec's closure to new clients, ChamberMaster's $3,900 starting price and GrowthZone's 4.4★, so confirm them before acting. Finally, **the scores are researcher judgments**: useful for ordering, not measurements.

## Conclusion

The decisive variable across ten tracks was **timing relative to a deal**, not niche size or how many competitors exist. Openings cluster one to four years after a roll-up buys the incumbent and before a modern challenger accumulates reviews; in niches where owner-operators shop online that window closed years ago, while in niches run by boards, volunteers and two-truck operators it stays open at the cost of slower sales. That shifts what a "clone" should be. The most reusable asset is not one product but a chassis that can be aimed at whichever roll-up announces the next acquisition: recurring billing that pays out to the customer's own merchant account, offline forms driven by a regulatory due-date engine, an importer for the incumbent's export, and a flat price.

The evidence also argues for spending the next dollar on buyers rather than on more desk research. Only two of the top twenty rest on Stripe-verified revenue, another track disputes one of those, and verified decline curves (Marky, LocalRank, Kortex) proved as informative as verified highs. A list of named buyers willing to pay for a concierge pilot is now worth more than another hundred Latka estimates, which is why every recommended test starts from a public licensee list, an association directory or an acquired product's user base rather than from paid ads.
