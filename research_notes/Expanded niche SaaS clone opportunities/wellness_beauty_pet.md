## Q1. Which incumbents prove there is money in these verticals, and how strong is that proof?

### Takeaway
Revenue proof is strong at the horizontal "salon/studio OS" layer: GlossGenius, Fresha, Boulevard, Vagaro, Booksy and Mindbody are each at tens to hundreds of $M. It is also strong in several sub-niches: Gymdesk (martial arts) was a bootstrapped solo-founder product that reached about $3M ARR and then took a PE deal; PE roll-up Togetherwork bought three pet-facility tools; PushPress and Wodify are at $10–15M (estimated); MoeGo raised a $24M Series A. Proof is weak to absent for tattoo-specific, equestrian, salon-suite, nail-turn and dog-trainer tools. There, the evidence is that the workflow exists and is monetised by small vendors at $35–$250/mo, not audited revenue.

### Inferences
- The proof that matters most for a solo founder is Gymdesk. It shows that a single founder with community credibility (a BJJ black belt) could take one martial-arts sub-niche to about $3M ARR without VC, against Zen Planner, Spark and Kicksite.
- The Togetherwork roll-up of Gingr, PetExec and Revelation Pets shows a PE exit path for pet-facility software. It also creates the classic post-roll-up neglect window (see Q2 #1).
- The TrustMRR pet tools are tiny. They show that indie entry is possible, not that it is lucrative. Do not use them as proof of scale.

### Gaps
- No Stripe-verified revenue was found for any tattoo, equestrian, climbing-gym, salon-suite, nail-salon or dog-trainer product.
- Latka had no pages (404) for MoeGo, Gingr, PetExec, Time To Pet, Revelation Pets, DaySmart, Arketa, Walla, Kicksite, Spark Membership or Porter.
- Squire (barbershop) funding and revenue were not verified this round.
