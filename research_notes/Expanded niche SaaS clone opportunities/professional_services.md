# Clone-and-improve vertical SaaS for US professional-services firms and small landlords: proven revenue, beatable incumbents, expansion paths

**Research date:** 2026-11-07.

**Method.** Capterra category, product and review pages were fetched live and parsed from the site's embedded JSON. Ratings, review counts and starting prices therefore come from the live listing; each review quote carries its own date. Latka pages, vendor pricing pages and the Census County Business Patterns 2023 US file were also fetched live. WebSearch was used for ownership history and industry counts until the session's search budget ran out near the end. One Tenstreet/DriverReach acquisition check could not be run, so it is listed as a gap.

**Provenance labels used throughout:**
- **[Capterra 11-07]**: the live Capterra listing, fetched 2026-11-07. Capterra's "starting price" field is sometimes misattributed, so it is flagged wherever it disagrees with the vendor page.
- **[vendor 11-07]**: the vendor's pricing page, fetched 2026-11-07.
- **[Latka est.]**: a third-party estimate shown with "Est." on Latka.
- **[Latka, not marked Est.]**: Latka shows the figure without "Est." It may be CEO-reported. It is unverified.
- **[vendor-claimed]**: a figure or claim from the company itself.
- **[secondary]**: an aggregator, listicle or competitor-written page.
- **[CBP 2023]**: Census County Business Patterns, data year 2023 (published 2025). Counts are **employer establishments only**, so solo operators with no employees are excluded.

The CBP source file for every count below is [Census CBP 2023 US file](https://www2.census.gov/programs-surveys/cbp/datasets/2023/cbp23us.zip). "<5 emp" means establishments with 1–4 employees.

**Scoring rubric.** Each shortlisted candidate gets 1–5 on five axes: revenue proof (R), beatability (B), copyability (C), expansion (E) and channel replicability (Ch), for a total out of 25. The scores are my inferences from the cited findings.

## Q0. Which candidates rank highest overall, and which were killed?

### Takeaway
I evaluated 26 candidates and shortlisted 8. The best are:
- **Independent self-storage and outdoor RV/boat storage ops (20/25).** Storable is a PE roll-up, and its acquired brands draw "Storable = Horrible" reviews.
- **Small-carrier driver hiring plus DQ-file compliance (20/25).** The niche is huge (91–95% of carriers run 10 or fewer trucks), with public FMCSA lead lists. But it already has many cheap entrants, and DriverReach now redirects to Tenstreet.
- **Interior-design procurement back-office (19/25).** Houzz Pro drew a cluster of 1★ reviews in February 2026, and Houzz wound down Ivy.

Next come manufactured-home-community management, land-surveying firm ops and private-investigator case management (18/25 each). Insurance-agency AMS and interpreting-agency ops score 16/25: revenue is proven, but they are harder to beat or build. Eighteen candidates were killed or deferred. In most of them a modern, well-rated, fairly priced product already leads the exact sub-niche: TaxDome, NextChapter, eImmigration/Docketwise, IRS Solutions, ServeManager, PayHOA and CASEpeer. In others, free tools set the price: TurboTenant, Innago and Stessa for small landlords.

### Cited Findings
- Shortlist scores (my scoring; the evidence is in each section below):

- Kill and defer reasons are listed in Q9 with citations.

### Inferences
- The common pattern in the top three is **PE or roll-up consolidation plus recent dated complaints, while the long tail of single-site or solo operators sits below the incumbents' sales focus.** That is beatability, not absence of competition.
- Where a cheap, well-rated, modern vertical tool already exists (TaxDome, NextChapter, ServeManager, PayHOA, eImmigration), the kill rule fired. These niches are not empty, but the incumbent is neither dated nor overpriced.

### Gaps
- Capterra review counts are a proxy for paying customers, not a revenue figure. No candidate had a Stripe-verified (TrustMRR) revenue figure in this vertical scope.
- Nonemployer (solo operator) counts were not pulled, so TAM for solo-heavy niches (PIs, interpreters, designers, surveyors) is understated.

## Q1. Shortlist #1: independent self-storage and outdoor RV/boat/vehicle storage ops software (score 20/25)

### Takeaway
Revenue is well proven. Storable, an EQT-majority-owned roll-up, absorbed SiteLink, storEDGE, SpareFoot and Easy Storage Solutions. Its legacy brands now draw dated complaints that service collapsed after acquisition. Lean, proven-abroad products show a small team can serve the segment: Storeganise (Latka est. $1.2M, 7 staff, $90/mo) and Stora (Sweden). The strongest wedge is **unmanned or outdoor RV/boat/container lots and 1–3 site independents**, combined with state-specific lien-sale automation.

- NAICS 531130 (lessors of miniwarehouses and self-storage units): 18,564 employer establishments, of which 16,965 have fewer than 5 employees [CBP 2023]

### Inferences
- **Proposed spin.** A "SiteLink escape hatch" for 1–3 site independents and **unmanned outdoor RV/boat/container lots**. Core features: online rent-and-move-in, e-sign lease, autopay, gate-code or smart-lock issuance, and a **state-aware lien ladder**. The ladder would cover notice templates, statutory timers, proof-of-delivery logging and auction-listing export.
- **MVP scope (about 8–12 weeks).** Unit and space map; online rental with Stripe ACH and card; tenant portal; delinquency ladder with per-state templates (start with the top 5 states); one or two access-control integrations or keypad-code lists; CSV import from SiteLink and ESS; basic occupancy and revenue reports.
- **Regulated-data notes.** PCI exposure is handled through Stripe. Lien-law templates need attorney review per state, which is also the moat.
- **Expansion path.**
  - Tenant protection or insurance revenue share.
  - Marketing website and AI chat leasing.
  - Payments margin.
  - Adjacent "specialty real estate": marinas (dry stack), RV long-term sites, parking lots, container yards and wine storage. Storable already validates this adjacency by selling Marine and RV & Campground products.
  - Multi-site management companies.
- **Channel.** Incumbents sell through the SSA, state associations and conferences (the SSA site lists chartered state associations and regional trade shows — [SSA site](https://www.selfstorage.org/)) and own the SpareFoot marketplace. A solo founder could replicate this with:
  - outbound to facility lists;
  - "switch from SiteLink, data migrated free" offers;
  - self-storage investor YouTube and Facebook communities (inference).
- **Scores.** R4 (PE roll-up + 700-review proxy + Latka est.), B4 (dated post-acquisition complaints; zero new Capterra reviews in 12 months for ESS and SiteLink suggests stagnation), C3 (payments + access-control integrations + lien templates), E5, Ch4.

### Gaps
- Storable, SiteLink and ESS current list prices were not captured; they are demo- or quote-driven.
- 6Storage's pricing page returned 404.
- Neither the share of facilities owned by single-site independents nor the count of outdoor RV/boat lots was found from a primary source.
- Whether major access-control vendors (gate keypads) offer open APIs to new entrants was not verified.

## Q2. Shortlist #2: small-carrier driver hiring + driver-qualification (DQ) file compliance (score 20/25)

### Takeaway
The original is Tenstreet: the recruiting and compliance standard for mid and large fleets. It already has a pay-as-you-go small-fleet tier, and **driverreach.com now 301-redirects to tenstreet.com** (observed 2026-11-07), which suggests consolidation. The US market is overwhelmingly tiny fleets, and FMCSA's regulations and registration data are public, so both copyability and channel are strong. The catch is that several cheap modern DQ-only tools exist (Roadworthy HQ $39–149/mo, DOTDriverFiles $50 per driver per year, CDLSuite from $199.95/mo). Winning here is about distribution plus bundling more compliance jobs, not just a better UI.

### Cited Findings
**Original product and revenue proof**
- Tenstreet: 2021 revenue $9.5M, 123 employees [Latka, not marked Est.; likely understated] — [Latka](https://getlatka.com/companies/tenstreet)
- G2 shows about 4.6/5 across roughly 21–23 reviews. The main complaint is complexity ("overwhelming complexity… hampers effective time management"). Pricing is unpublished; one third-party range of $300 to $2,500+ per month is explicitly an estimate [secondary] — [G2 Tenstreet reviews](https://www.g2.com/products/tenstreet/reviews); [SoftwareFinder](https://softwarefinder.com/fleet-management-software/tenstreet)
- Tenstreet's site markets a small-fleets path ("Pay for services to hire and manage drivers only when you need them"), plus its IntelliApp application and Xchange verification products [vendor 11-07] — [Tenstreet](https://www.tenstreet.com/)
- **driverreach.com returns HTTP 301 → https://www.tenstreet.com/** (curl, 2026-11-07). DriverReach has no Capterra rating — [DriverReach Capterra](https://www.capterra.com/p/276221/DriverReach/)

**Down-market incumbents (cheap and modern, few reviews)**
- Roadworthy HQ, "aimed at small US motor carriers" [vendor 11-07]. Solo plan: 1 driver, $39/mo. Fleet: 2–5 drivers, $89/mo. Fleet+: 6–10 drivers, $149/mo. "No quote, no contract, no consultant." Covers driver, drug and alcohol, vehicle and accident files — [Roadworthy HQ pricing](https://roadworthyhq.com/pricing)
- DOTDriverFiles [vendor 11-07]: a free tier that charges only for reports; Pro at $50 per driver per year; Pro+ at $50 per driver plus $20 per vehicle per year. MVRs from $12.95 and PSP at $19.99 — [DOTDriverFiles pricing](https://www.dotdriverfiles.com/pricing)
- CDLSuite: "starting at only $199.95/month" for recruiting plus DQ [vendor 11-07]. Capterra: 4.8★ from 28 reviews, newest February 16, 2026 — [CDLSuite pricing](https://cdlsuite.com/pricing.php); [Capterra](https://www.capterra.com/p/196259/CDLSuite/reviews/)
- FleetCollect lists a solo plan around $14/mo with a free plan, but the listing may be stale [secondary] — [GetApp](https://www.getapp.com/all-software/a/fleetcollect/)

**US market size**
- Secondary summaries of FMCSA data put the share of carriers operating 10 or fewer trucks at about 95.5% (March 2024) or about 91.5% (August 2025 MCMIS snapshot). The difference likely reflects the denominator [secondary, conflicting] — [Linxup](https://www.linxup.com/blog/trucking-industry-statistics); [ELDT Nation](https://eldtnation.com/blog/how-many-trucking-companies-are-in-the-u-s-in-2026)
- NAICS 484121 (long-distance truckload): 60,137 employer establishments, 46,818 with fewer than 5 employees. NAICS 484110 (local general freight): 46,302, of which 35,947 have fewer than 5 employees [CBP 2023]

### Inferences
- **Proposed spin.** "Hire and stay audit-ready" for 1–25 truck carriers, priced per active driver. The bundle:
  - an FMCSA-compliant online application (49 CFR 391.21 fields);
  - previous-employer safety-performance requests;
  - an auto-built DQ file with annual MVR review, medical-card expiry and Clearinghouse-query reminders;
  - one-click audit binder export.
  
  The differentiation from Roadworthy HQ and DOTDriverFiles is bundling **recruiting (job posting + application) with compliance**, which the cheap DQ tools mostly lack. CDLSuite does it at $199.95/mo.
- **MVP scope (about 6–10 weeks).** Application form and e-sign; applicant pipeline; DQ checklist engine; document upload with OCR for CDL and medical cards; expiry reminders; audit-binder PDF; MVR and PSP ordering through a reseller API.
- **Regulated data.** Driver PII, MVRs and drug-test results call for FCRA-style handling, and Clearinghouse access rules apply. No certification is required to ship.
- **Expansion path.**
  - Drug and alcohol consortium enrolment through a C/TPA partner.
  - IFTA/UCR/BOC-3/MCS-150 filing reminders.
  - Insurance-agent referrals (DQ hygiene affects premiums).
  - MVR and PSP resale margin.
  - A job-board marketplace for owner-operators.
  - Upmarket to 25–100 truck fleets that find Tenstreet heavy.
- **Channel.** FMCSA new-authority registrations are public, which allows daily outbound to brand-new carriers (inference; registration data is what the secondary statistics cite). Also trucking Facebook groups and YouTube. Incumbents appear to rely on direct sales, partnerships and schools; Tenstreet's site names "Schools" as a segment [vendor].
- **Scores.** R3, B3 (incumbent complexity, but crowded cheap entrants), C4, E5, Ch5.

### Gaps
- The Tenstreet–DriverReach transaction could not be confirmed with a press source (the web-search budget ran out). Only the redirect was observed.
- Small-fleet churn and willingness to pay above $5 per driver per month were not found.
- FMCSA's own registration dashboard was not fetched directly.

## Q3. Shortlist #3: interior-design procurement + client billing for 1–5 person studios (score 19/25)

### Takeaway
Revenue is proven by large review bases: Houzz Pro (1,089 reviews) and DesignFiles (533). Studio Designer, the legacy per-seat back-office, costs $64–99 per user per month. The incumbents are visibly beatable:
- Houzz Pro drew a cluster of 1★ reviews in February 2026 over onboarding, contracts, invoicing and support.
- Houzz stopped selling Ivy and froze its features.
- Mydoma users report weekly glitches since its 2.0 rewrite.
- DesignFiles got a June 2025 1★ titled "Not Ready for Professional Use."

Programa, Australian-built and now selling in the US (4.8★, $71/mo plus $31 per seat), shows a modern entrant can win. That makes it a fast-follower target, not an empty niche.

## Q4. Shortlist #4: mom-and-pop manufactured-home-community (MHP) management (score 18/25)

### Takeaway
The niche is big: 43,000+ communities and about 4.3M homesites per MHI. Its workflow differs from generic rentals because the home and the lot are separate assets, and utility billing uses submeters or RUBS. Today it is served by heavy generalists (Rent Manager, Yardi Breeze), utility-billing add-ons (Zego) and one MH-only vendor that sells through demos (ManageAmerica). Revenue proof is indirect (review counts), and beatability rests on complexity, hidden costs and support complaints.

### Cited Findings
**Market**
- MHI: "more than 43,000 manufactured home communities in the U.S., with about 4.3 million homesites" — [MHI](https://www.manufacturedhousing.org/industry-resources/community-research/manufactured-housing-communities-in-the-u-s/). Freddie Mac (2019) estimated about 45,000, and a Duke paper uses about 50,000 [secondary] — [Freddie Mac](https://mf.freddiemac.com/docs/dts_mhroc_report.pdf); [Duke](https://dukespace.lib.duke.edu/server/api/core/bitstreams/4b6734ff-c53e-4f8f-8795-f5b934dcca2b/content)
- NAICS 531190 (lessors of other real estate property): 8,957 employer establishments, 7,266 with fewer than 5 employees [CBP 2023]. This is a broader code, and its MH share is not isolated.

**Incumbents and workflow**
- ManageAmerica: "The only property management software built exclusively for manufactured housing… Clear home & site distinction… Specialized utility management." Demo-request driven, with no public price found [vendor-claimed, fetched 11-07] — [ManageAmerica](https://www.manageamerica.com/). No Capterra reviews — [Capterra](https://www.capterra.com/p/10003839/ManageAmerica/reviews/)
- Rent Manager treats the home and the lot as separate leased assets. Utility billing models in parks are flat fee, RUBS or submetering [vendor/secondary] — [Rent Manager MH](https://www.rentmanager.com/?p=50264); [Yardi on MH utility billing](https://www.yardi.com/blog/utility-billing-manufactured-housing/)
- Capterra [11-07]:
  - Rent Manager: 4.63★ from 662 reviews, 127 in the last 12 months.
  - Yardi Breeze: 4.22★ from 337 reviews.
  - Zego: 4.54★ from 91 reviews.
  - Rentmoji: 4.91★ from 11 reviews.
  - DoorLoop: 4.83★ from 719 reviews (a generalist).
  
  Source: [Capterra mobile-home-park category](https://www.capterra.com/mobile-home-park-management-software/)
- DoorLoop (a generalist) markets MHP management "starting at just $59 monthly for 20 units" [vendor-claimed] — [DoorLoop blog](https://www.doorloop.com/blog/mobile-home-park-management-software)

### Inferences
- Several "kills" still have unhappy users: Docketwise's billing complaints, Best Case's decline, ServeManager's price rise. Those are better approached as **niche marketing against a single incumbent** (for example "Docketwise alternative, flat price") than as new products, because a well-rated, cheap alternative already exists for buyers to switch to.
- The shortlist skews toward the "small landlords and specialty real estate" and "field-crew professional services" ends of the scope. Law and accounting practice software is the most mature and best-rated part of the US professional-services stack.

### Gaps
- Not researched this round: AI petition and evidence-packet builders for EB-2 NIW/O-1 immigration filings (a possible sub-niche inside the killed immigration category), nonprofit DOJ-accredited immigration providers, and Section 8 or rent-by-the-room landlord tooling.
- Financial-planner portals and healthcare-specific staffing ATSs need a dedicated pass.
- The web-search budget ran out before the Tenstreet/DriverReach and Vertafore/QQCatalyst deals could be confirmed with primary press releases.
